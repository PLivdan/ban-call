Methods: text and four figures

A rewrite of the main page's "How it works" section. The text is the same for every option. Pick one figure (or none). Nothing here is on the main page yet.

The text

What the text no longer says

  • which kinds of models are fitted, their terms and features
  • how a ban's value is computed, and in what order
  • the network sizes, how many are trained, and how the page's copy is made
  • the support threshold, the caution rule and the stand-in sampling
  • the targeting term and the placebo test behind it
  • the draft calibration and how drafts are simulated
  • which tests are run on the model, and their rules
  • run names and model versions (they stay in the page's footer line)

Four figures

All four show that the model has many parts and plays lobbies out at scale. None shows how a part works. Numbers come from the live model.

M1

The pipeline: from real matches to your page

Sealed boxes: what each stage learns is visible, how it learns it is not.

For: the clearest picture of how much sits behind one ban, left to right. Against: the most familiar kind of diagram, so it impresses least.

M2

The ban phase as a tree

Every ban opens new branches. Yours are decisions (blue), theirs are forecasts (red). Each branch end is drafted and scored many times.

For: shows the real problem, what makes it hard, and why the page can't just look it up. Against: abstract, and the tree is drawn at a tiny fraction of its real size.

M3

Orders of magnitude

Log scale: each grid line is ten times the one before. Everything collapses to one number per ban on your screen.

For: scale is the most impressive and the least revealing thing we can show. Against: says nothing about structure, only size.

M4

What depends on what

What you enter (blue) feeds what the model works out (grey), which feeds what you see. Hover a box to follow its links.

For: conveys the interactions, which are the hard part, without a single formula. Against: the busiest to read, and it needs the hover to untangle.
